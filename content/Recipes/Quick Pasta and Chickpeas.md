## Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 2 cloves garlic, peeled and smashed
- 3 tablespoons tomato paste (Wendy: use the entire little can when doubling)
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper or red pepper flakes, to taste
- 1½ cups cooked chickpeas (from one 15-ounce can, drained and rinsed; Wendy saves the chickpea liquid to add to the recipe)
- ½ cup (2 ounces or 55 grams) uncooked ditalini pasta (or another small shape, such as annelini)
- 2 cups (475 ml) boiling water (or tap water)
- A parmesan rind, added while simmering (Wendy's addition)
- A couple handfuls of baby spinach, added towards the end to wilt (Wendy's addition)

### To finish
- 2 to 3 tablespoons olive oil (estimate 1 per serving)
- 1 clove of garlic, peeled and finely chopped
- 1 teaspoon minced fresh rosemary
- Salt and red pepper flakes

## Directions

- In a medium-large heavy-bottomed pot or deep sauté pan, heat 2 tablespoons olive oil until it shimmers. Add 2 smashed cloves of garlic and cook, stirring, until it becomes lightly, barely browned but very fragrant. Stir in the tomato paste, salt, and pepper and cook them with the garlic for 30 seconds or so. Add the chickpeas, pasta, and boiling water (add the parmesan rind here if using). Stir to scrape up any browned bits on the bottom of the pot, lower the heat, and simmer until the pasta is cooked and a lot of the liquid has been absorbed, about 15 to 20 minutes (stir in spinach near the end to wilt, if using). Taste and adjust seasoning and ladle into bowls.
- Make finishing oil: Heat 2 to 3 tablespoons olive oil in a small sauce- or frying pan over medium-low heat with remaining clove of garlic, rosemary, a pinch or two of salt and pepper flakes, until sizzling; pull it off the heat as soon as the garlic is going to start taking on color. Drizzle this over bowls of pasta e ceci and eat it right away.

## Notes

- Want to amp it up a little? Use the broth from freshly cooked chickpeas instead of water. You could also add a parmesan rind to the cooking liquid (this negates the vegan/vegetarian label). Recipe adapted, just a little, from Victoria Granof.
- Wendy's handwritten notes, dated 1.9.2024, signed "-WN": add a parmesan rind while simmering; add a couple handfuls of baby spinach towards the end to wilt; made often during quarantine 2020. When doubling the recipe, add one extra cup of pasta (she used wagon wheels) and use the entire little can of tomato paste. Save the chickpea cooking water and add it to the recipe. Definitely make lots of the crispy garlic finishing oil.
