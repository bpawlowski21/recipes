Total time: 1 hour 25 minutes.

This is the pie dough recipe used for every tart, pie, and galette. Shows how to make the pie dough as well as how to parbake and blind bake it — parbaking ensures the bottom crust won't be soggy. Recipes that benefit from a parbaked crust: Bourbon Pecan Pie, Roasted Butternut Squash Pie (i.e. The Best Pumpkin Pie), Salted Maple Pie.

## Ingredients

- 2½ cups (320g) all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons (25g) sugar
- ½ teaspoon (3g) kosher salt
- 16 tablespoons (227g) unsalted butter, cut into small pieces and chilled
- ½ cup + 2 tablespoons (142g) ice water

**For baking and storing:** pie weights or dried beans/rice, parchment paper, jumbo (2-gallon) Ziploc

## Directions

1. In the bowl of a food processor, pulse the flour, sugar, and salt together. Add the chilled, cubed butter. Pulse at 1-second intervals until butter is the size of peas — about ten 1-second pulses. Add the ice water and pulse again about 10 times until the mixture is crumbly but holds together when pinched. (To make without a food processor: whisk flour, sugar, and salt together in a large bowl. Add the butter and incorporate with a fork or pastry cutter until in small pieces. Add ice water and stir with a fork until crumbly but holds together when pinched.)
2. Lay two clean tea towels on a work surface. Dump half of the crumbly dough mixture into the center of each. Grab the four corners of the towel together and twist to create a beggar's purse, pressing the dough into a round. Use your hands to pack and flatten the round. Wrap in plastic wrap or parchment paper, place in a ziplock or other airtight bag/vessel, and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes, ideally 1 hour, or up to 3 days. Can also be frozen for up to 3 months.
3. **To parbake or blind bake the pie crust:** Heat oven to 425°F. If using a Baking Steel or pizza stone, place it on a rack in the lower third of the oven. On a lightly floured surface, roll out one chilled round of dough, flipping every few strokes, until it's roughly 15 inches in diameter — the thickness should be about that of a Ritz cracker.
4. Transfer the round to a pie plate. Trim overhanging dough to leave roughly ½ inch overhang (save scraps in the fridge). Tuck the overhang behind itself and crimp into a fluted pattern. Lay a sheet of parchment across the pie plate and pour pie weights or dried beans into the center until they reach the top of the crust, pressing them into the fluted edges.
5. Transfer the filled pie crust to a parchment-lined sheet pan and bake for 15–20 minutes, or until the edges are just beginning to color.
6. Remove the pan from the oven and carefully remove the pie weights or beans (let them cool completely, then store for reuse). Return the pan to the oven. For a **parbaked** crust, bake 2–3 more minutes — it should take on only the slightest bit more color. For a **blind-baked** crust, bake 10–12 more minutes, until evenly golden brown.
7. Let the crust cool completely, then store in an airtight vessel or bag — an XLarge Ziploc works well — at room temperature for up to 3 days. Proceed with whichever pie recipe you are making.

## Notes

- Dough can be made up to three days in advance and stored in the fridge, or made weeks in advance and stored in the freezer. Thaw in the fridge overnight before baking.
- To make a double batch: don't load the food processor with double quantities — make the dough in separate batches (no need to wash the processor in between).
- Favorite pie plate: ceramic Emile Henry 9-inch pie plate.
