"Basque cheesecake, (also known as burnt cheesecake), originated in Spain and has become an internet sensation with its blackened top and outrageously creamy interior. Believe it or not, it's also the easiest cheesecake recipe on my blog with only 5 minutes of prep and no water bath!"

Prep Time: 10 minutes · Cook Time: 35 minutes · Cooling Time: 5 hours · Total Time: 5 hours 45 minutes · Calories: 575

Substantial handwritten additions on the card. Original scan:

![[Basque Cheesecake (Burnt Cheesecake) (annotated).pdf]]

**Equipment:** 9" springform pan, 72 oz capacity blender (see note 1 if you don't have a blender that large), parchment paper

## Ingredients

- 32 oz (907g) full-fat Philadelphia cream cheese, cold
- 2 cups (400g) granulated sugar
- 1 cup (236ml) heavy cream, cold
- 4 large eggs, cold
- 2 teaspoon vanilla extract
- ¾ teaspoon salt
- *(handwritten addition: zest of ½ lemon or orange, for citrus flavor)*

## Directions

1. Arrange rack to middle of the oven (see note 2) and preheat oven to 325°F (165°C).
2. Line a 9" (23cm) springform pan all the way up the sides with parchment paper. The parchment should extend at least an inch above the sides of the pan — the author usually uses two perpendicular pieces of parchment to achieve this.
3. Combine ALL ingredients in a blender and blend until completely smooth. May need to stop and scrape the sides — want to make sure there are no lumps of cream cheese that have settled on the sides or bottom.
4. Pour batter evenly into prepared baking pan. Repeatedly and firmly tap the pan against your countertop to help remove air bubbles. *(handwritten: let rest on counter 20 minutes — this will help air bubbles escape)*
5. Transfer to 325°F (165°C) preheated oven and bake for 35 minutes. Once time has elapsed, do not open the oven but increase oven temperature to 500°F (260°C) (see note 3 if your oven doesn't go this high) and bake for approximately 5-10 minutes until the top of the cheesecake is deeply and thoroughly browned (watch the cheesecake through the glass with the oven light on — exactly how long this will take varies by individual oven, but don't want the cheesecake to be completely charred).
6. Remove from oven — it should still be extremely jiggly. Allow to cool to room temperature, then transfer to refrigerator to chill overnight or for at least 5 hours before removing the ring from the springform pan, peeling back the parchment, slicing and enjoying.

## Notes

- The San Sebastian Basque cheesecake is often served at room temperature — can let the slices come to room temperature before serving, but the author personally thinks this cheesecake is best cold.
- **1) If You Don't Have A Blender:** can be made with an electric mixer instead — use softened cream cheese. Stir together cream cheese and sugar until smooth, scrape the bottom of the bowl repeatedly and each time an ingredient is added. Slowly beat in heavy cream, vanilla, and salt, slowly increasing speed to high and beating for 15 seconds. Whisk eggs together in a bowl and with mixer on low speed gradually pour eggs into mixture. Increase mixer speed to high and beat until batter is smooth and completely combined. Bake as instructed.
- **2) Oven:** this recipe was tested and developed with a conventional US oven — not tested in a fan/convection oven.
- **3) Oven temperature:** if your oven doesn't go to 500°F (260°C), simply turn it to the highest temperature it allows — may need a bit more than 10 minutes in the oven.
- **Storing:** can leave the cheesecake in the springform pan it was baked in — just cover with plastic wrap to help keep it from absorbing any odors in the fridge. The author leaves hers in the springform pan until ready to serve, removes the ring to slice and serve, then replaces the ring and re-covers to store leftovers.
