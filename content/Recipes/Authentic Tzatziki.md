Handwritten corrections on the printed card (a different, distinct Tzatziki recipe from the one already in the vault — this one is by Eli K. Giannopoulos, uses 500g strained Greek yogurt, olive oil, and red wine vinegar). Original scan:

![[Authentic Tzatziki (handwritten).pdf]]

Prep Time: 10 min · Cook Time: 0 min · Total Time: 10 minutes · Yield: 3 cups

## Ingredients

- 1 cucumber, remove skin & seeds, grate on large holes of box grater *(handwritten correction — printed card originally just said "1 cucumber")*
- 2 cloves of garlic, minced or grated *(handwritten addition: "or grated")*
- 1/4 of a cup extra virgin olive oil
- 500g strained Greek yogurt (18 ounces) *(handwritten correction: "Greek yogurt doesn't need to be strained")*
- 1–2 tbsps of red wine vinegar
- a pinch of salt and freshly ground pepper

## Directions

1. To prepare this authentic Greek tzatziki sauce recipe, remove the skin and the seeds of the cucumber and grate it into a large bowl. Season with salt and pepper and leave aside for 10 minutes.
2. Afterwards, wrap the grated cucumber in a towel and squeeze, in order to get rid of the excess water.
3. Assemble the tzatziki sauce. In a bowl, add the cucumber, the minced garlic and olive oil, the yogurt, 1-2 tbsps of red wine vinegar, a pinch of salt and blend, until the ingredients are combined.
4. Store the tzatziki sauce in the fridge and always serve cold. (Alternatively, if you have a blender, pour in the olive oil and garlic and blend until combined. Then mix with the rest of the ingredients using a spoon. This will help the garlic to mix better with the rest of the ingredients).
5. Prepare this traditional tzatziki sauce for your friends and family and serve as a delicious dip with some warm pita breads aside, or as a sauce for Greek-style souvlaki. Enjoy!

## Notes

- Handwritten corrections on the scanned card (in red pen, signed "un"): remove the cucumber's skin and seeds and grate it on the large holes of a box grater; garlic can be minced or grated; and — most notably — a correction to the printed recipe stating Greek yogurt doesn't actually need to be strained (contradicting the printed ingredient line "500g strained Greek yogurt").
- Distinct from the "Tzatziki" recipe already in the vault (a different, simpler Apple Notes–sourced recipe using dill and no olive-oil-blending step) — kept as a separate note since the ingredients and method differ meaningfully.
