Brown-butter chocolate chip cookies, crisp at the edges and gooey in the center. 5 stars from 366 reviews.

Prep Time: 15 minutes · Cook Time: 11 minutes · Total Time: 26 minutes · Yield: 16 cookies

## Ingredients

- ¾ cup (168 g) unsalted butter
- 1 cup (200 g) brown sugar, packed
- ¼ cup (50 g) granulated sugar
- 1 egg + 1 egg yolk, room temperature
- 1 tablespoon pure vanilla extract
- 1¾ cup (220 g) all-purpose flour
- ¾ teaspoon baking soda
- ¾ teaspoon kosher salt, plus more flaky sea salt for sprinkling
- 1½ cups semisweet chocolate, chopped

**Equipment:** baking sheets, cookie scoop, hand mixer (cordless), parchment paper sheets, rubber spatula, sauce pan

## Directions

1. Brown the butter over medium heat, stirring constantly until the butter begins to foam and turns a golden brown, emitting a nutty aroma. Only brown it lightly — as soon as it starts to turn brown and smell nutty, take it off the heat to prevent more liquid from evaporating out (too much evaporation can dry out the dough). Allow to cool.
2. In a large mixing bowl, combine the cooled brown butter, brown sugar, and white sugar. Beat until mixed together. Add in the egg, egg yolk, and vanilla extract. Mix well.
3. In a separate bowl, mix together the flour, salt and baking soda. Mix half the dry ingredients into the wet until combined. Slowly add in the remaining flour a little at a time, stopping if the dough starts to get too dry. Fold in the chocolate. Do not over mix.
4. Refrigerate the cookie dough for at least a half hour, or overnight.
5. When ready to bake, preheat the oven to 350°F and line a cookie sheet with parchment paper. Use a 1.5 or 2-ounce cookie scoop to scoop the dough into balls, placing them 2 inches apart on the prepared sheet. Bake for 11 minutes, or until the edges are just golden brown and the centers have puffed up but are still gooey.
6. Allow to cool before eating!

## Notes

- If aiming for precision, the author's butter is always at 140 grams after being browned — if you have less left over, add milk until it reaches 140 grams.
- Browned butter can evaporate off at different levels, so you may be left with less liquid than others — add the flour in increments to avoid ending up with a dry dough.
- Every oven is different — take the cookies out right when they've puffed up and *just* turned golden brown on the edges. The cookies should still be raw in the middle; past that point they won't have the gooey center this recipe is going for.
