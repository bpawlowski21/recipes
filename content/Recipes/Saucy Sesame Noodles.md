Printed from a Google "AI Overview" search result rather than a blog or cookbook. "This recipe for Saucy Sesame Noodles produces a rich, creamy, and slightly spicy dish in about 15-20 minutes. The key to making them exceptionally saucy is using a combination of peanut butter (or tahini) and starchy noodle water to create an emulsified, clingy sauce."

## Ingredients

**The Noodles**
- 1 lb (16 oz) linguine, spaghetti, ramen, or udon noodles
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (for tossing after cooking)

**The Creamy Sesame Sauce**
- ¼ cup smooth peanut butter or Chinese sesame paste (or a mix of both)
- ¼ cup soy sauce (or tamari)
- 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil
- 2 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tbsp chili oil or sriracha (add more for extra spice)
- 1-2 tbsp sugar, honey, or maple syrup (to balance the savory)
- 2-3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
- ¼ to ½ cup hot water (reserved from cooking the noodles)

**Garnish (Optional but recommended)**
- Sliced scallions (green onions)
- Toasted sesame seeds
- Crushed peanuts
- Freshly julienned cucumber

## Directions

1. **Cook the Noodles:** bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the noodles according to package directions until al dente. Before draining, reserve about 1 cup of the starchy noodle water.
2. **Make the Sauce:** while the noodles cook, whisk together the peanut butter/sesame paste, soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, rice vinegar, chili oil/sriracha, sugar, garlic, and ginger in a large bowl.
3. **Create the "Saucy" Texture:** slowly add ¼ cup of the hot, reserved noodle water into the sauce mixture while whisking. Continue adding water until the sauce is creamy, smooth, and coats the back of a spoon.
4. **Combine:** drain the noodles (do not rinse if serving warm, rinse with cold water if serving cold) and immediately add them to the bowl with the sauce. Toss vigorously until the noodles are fully coated.
5. **Garnish and Serve:** top with sliced green onions, toasted sesame seeds, crushed peanuts, and cucumber.

## Notes

- Best noodles: thin wheat noodles, linguine, or ramen work best for grabbing the sauce.
- Prevent sticking: if serving cold, rinse the noodles in cold water after cooking and toss with 1 tbsp of sesame oil to keep them from clumping.
- Make it a meal: add grilled chicken, shrimp, fried tofu, or sautéed bok choy.
- Storage: can be stored in the fridge for up to 4 days, though the sauce will thicken as it cools — add a splash of water when reheating.
