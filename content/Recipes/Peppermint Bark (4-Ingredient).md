"Made with only four ingredients, this peppermint bark is one of the simplest holiday cookies you could make and one of the most festive, too. What's more, it yields a ton, making it perfect for gifting all season long. So easy, so pretty, so delicious!" Adapted from Dinner at The Zoo's recipe, with small changes to the method (stovetop + double boiler to melt the chocolates) and to the candy canes (blitzed into dust rather than coarsely chopped).

Total time: 1 hour

## Ingredients

- 12 ounces bittersweet chocolate (the author used Guittard bittersweet 74% cacao chocolate wafers)
- 16 ounces white chocolate, finely chopped
- ½ teaspoon peppermint extract, divided
- 4 regular candy canes

## Directions

1. Fill two small pots with water halfway full. Bring to a simmer.
2. Place the dark chocolate in a small bowl. Place the white chocolate in a small bowl. Set each bowl over a pot and leave undisturbed until you see the chocolate beginning to melt, about 5 minutes.
3. While the chocolates melt slowly on the stovetop, unwrap the candy canes, then place in a food processor. Purée until the candy canes are nearly dust or until they are chopped to a coarseness of your liking.
4. Stir the dark chocolate with a spatula until smooth, then add ¼ teaspoon of peppermint extract, and stir until completely smooth.
5. Line a 9×13-inch or 10×14-inch pan with parchment paper. Spread the dark chocolate into the pan, then transfer to the fridge for 10 to 12 minutes or until the chocolate just sets — note: due to some user reviews, rely on visual cues here, the chocolate should be visually set, but start checking at 10 minutes (glass pans may take longer). Don't leave the chocolate in the fridge much beyond the point at which it's set, otherwise the white chocolate risks not adhering to the chocolate layer; if the bottom layer hasn't set, however, the white chocolate will swirl into it. May take some trial and error to get the timing right for your fridge/pan.
6. Stir the white chocolate with a spatula until smooth, then add the remaining ¼ teaspoon of peppermint extract, and stir until completely smooth.
7. Spread the melted white chocolate over the dark chocolate — can do this with a spatula, the back of a spoon, or a small offset spatula. Sprinkle roughly ¼ cup of the peppermint dust over the surface, using a sieve or by simply scattering it over the surface with your hands.
8. Return to the fridge for 20 minutes. Remove from the fridge and let sit at room temperature for another 20 minutes.
9. Cut into neat triangles or break into shards. To cut into triangles, first trim away the rough edges, then cut the bar roughly into 2-inch squares, finally cut each square in half on the diagonal to create triangles. Return the bark to the fridge for another 20 minutes to firm up.
10. Store the bark at room temperature in an airtight container or in the fridge for up to 3 weeks.

## Notes

- **Chocolate:** the author uses Guittard bittersweet 74% cacao chocolate wafers, tasty on its own — important since it'll be paired with sweet white chocolate, which will offset the bitterness somewhat. Don't use a chocolate too sweet like milk chocolate; pick a tasty, dark-ish chocolate.
- **White Chocolate:** recommends Callebaut, Valrhona, or Guittard; otherwise use what you can find. Ghirardelli is readily available and works well here too. Do not use white chocolate chips as they will not melt well.
- **Candy Canes:** the author likes to pulse the candy canes until blitzed into dust — you'll only need about 4 candy canes for this recipe, but she'd recommend blitzing more so you have the dust on hand for other purposes like more bark or rolling sugar cookies in before baking.
- **The Pan:** need a 9×13-inch or 10×14-inch pan for this — the author has been loving her 10×14-inch Lloyd Detroit-Style pizza pan for this recipe.
