## Ingredients

### Crust
- 2 cups (240g) King Arthur Unbleached All-Purpose Flour
- 3/4 teaspoon table salt
- 1/2 teaspoon instant yeast or active dry yeast
- 3/4 cup (170g) lukewarm water
- 1 tablespoon (13g) olive oil, plus 1 1/2 tablespoons (18g) for the pan

### Topping
- 6 ounces (170g) grated mozzarella cheese (about 1 1/4 cups, loosely packed)
- 1/3 to 1/2 cup (74g to 113g) tomato sauce or pizza sauce
- Fresh grated hard cheese (Parmesan, Asiago, or Romano) and fresh herbs (oregano, basil, thyme) for finishing, optional

## Directions

- Weigh flour or measure by spooning gently into a cup and sweeping off excess.
- Combine flour, salt, yeast, water, and 1 tablespoon olive oil in a mixer bowl or medium-large mixing bowl. Stir until no dry flour remains, forming a shaggy, sticky mass. Cover the bowl.
- After 5 minutes, fold the dough by stretching it from bottom up and over the top four times, rotating the bowl 90° between each stretch. Re-cover and repeat every 5 minutes for a total of four folds.
- Let rest, covered, for 40 minutes, then refrigerate for a minimum of 12 hours (up to 72 hours).
- Three hours before serving, oil a 10-11" cast iron skillet (Wendy uses her purple Le Creuset sauté pan instead) with 1 1/2 tablespoons olive oil.
- Transfer chilled dough to the oiled pan, turn to coat both sides, and press/dimple with fingertips toward the edges, resting 15 minutes between attempts if the dough resists, until it reaches the pan edges.
- Cover the crust and let rise 2 hours at room temperature until soft, pillowy, and jiggly.
- About 30 minutes before baking, position oven racks (one at the bottom, one 4-5" from the top) and preheat to 450°F.
- Oil the top of the pan again before sprinkling with cheese. Scatter three-quarters of the mozzarella evenly over the crust, dollop small spoonfuls of sauce over the cheese, then sprinkle remaining mozzarella.
- Bake on the bottom rack for 18-20 minutes until cheese bubbles and crust edges are golden brown, moving to the top rack for a few minutes if the bottom browns before the top (or vice versa).
- Remove from oven, run a knife/spatula around the edge, and transfer to a cooling rack or cutting surface promptly to prevent sogginess.
- Serve warm to medium-hot; cut with kitchen shears or scissors.
