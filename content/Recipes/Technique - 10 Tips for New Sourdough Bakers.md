## Key Tips

- **Flour flexibility** — different flours work for starters; adjust water ratios accordingly. Whole grains provide an initial boost but aren't required.
- **Container materials** — plastic bowls and metal spoons are fine. Avoid cast iron or uncoated aluminum that react with acidity.
- **Water quality** — tap water works; bottled or spring water isn't necessary. Let heavily chlorinated tap water sit overnight.
- **Starter dormancy** — a temporarily inactive starter isn't dead; bacterial transitions cause natural rest periods. Continue regular feedings.
- **Discard reduction** — excess starter needn't be wasted; share with friends, use in recipes, or maintain a smaller culture.
- **Storage container** — use a lidded vessel (glass, plastic, or stoneware) large enough for triple expansion; lid shouldn't be airtight.
- **Bubbles vs. rise** — small bubbles alone don't indicate readiness; starter must double within 6-8 hours to effectively leaven bread.
- **Starter revival** — starters rarely die; neglected cultures recover with consistent feeding. Only discard if mold or pink/orange discoloration appears.
- **Baking equipment** — bannetons, Dutch ovens, and rice flour are helpful but unnecessary for successful sourdough baking.
- **Beginner recipes** — "Rustic Sourdough Bread" includes commercial yeast for reliability; "Naturally Leavened Sourdough Bread" requires a stronger starter.
