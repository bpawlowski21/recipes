## Ingredients

### Peach Filling
- 5-8 large fresh yellow peaches, sliced (do not use canned peaches)
- ¼ cup sugar

### Cobbler Topping
- ½ cup salted butter, melted
- ¾ cup sugar (Wendy increases to roughly 1½ cups or more, per handwritten note)
- 1 teaspoon vanilla
- 1 cup flour
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- A pinch of salt
- ¼ cup turbinado sugar (optional, but really nice)
- Whipped cream or vanilla ice cream for serving

## Directions

- Preheat the oven to 350 degrees. Smear the bottom of a 9×13 pan with a little bit of butter. Add peaches and sugar, and mix directly in the pan.
- Mix the butter, sugar, vanilla, flour, baking powder, and salt until a cookie-dough-like batter forms.
- Place scoops of the mixture over the top of the peaches. Spread with a spoon or with your fingers — no need for perfection here.
- Bake for 30 minutes. At this point it should be juicy and bubbly around the sides — if not, see notes.
- Sprinkle the top with turbinado sugar. Bake another 10 minutes and then finish with a few minutes under the broiler (475-ish) to get it golden brown and slightly crunchy on top.
- To get a thick, saucy peach base, let it stand for 20-30 minutes before serving with some vanilla ice cream.

## Notes

- "I have tried so many peach cobbler recipes and this is by far the BEST peach cobbler! Made with fresh peaches, sugar, and a topping that bakes like slightly underbaked cookie dough, with crunchy sugar broiled on top."
- The source page's printed notes section on if your peaches aren't getting saucy is cut off partway through in this scan ("...it's probably because your" — sentence doesn't continue on the scanned page); flagging as incomplete.
- Wendy's handwritten notes: peeling tip — make an "X" in the bottom of each peach, place in boiling water for 30 seconds, and the skin will peel right off (peaches shouldn't be overly ripe). Dated 7.11.22: use a rectangular white porcelain dish, not oval. Additional notes: do not use canned peaches; peel or don't peel, your choice; can add any mix of fruit (berries, nectarines, etc.).
