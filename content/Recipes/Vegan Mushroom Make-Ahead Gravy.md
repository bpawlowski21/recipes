## Ingredients

- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil (Wendy's amendment to the printed 1/2 cup)
- 1 small onion, finely chopped (1/2 cup), plus 1 minced garlic clove (Wendy's addition)
- 8 ounces baby portobello mushrooms, finely chopped (2 cups), plus dried porcini (soaked) — Wendy's addition
- 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
- 3 cups vegetable stock, preferably homemade, as needed
- 2 tablespoons soy sauce, more to taste (Wendy's amendment to the printed 1 teaspoon)
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
- 1/4 teaspoon black pepper

## Directions

1. In a large skillet, heat oil over medium-high heat. Add onion and mushrooms; cook, stirring, until well browned, 8 to 10 minutes.
2. Sprinkle in flour and cook, stirring, until golden brown, 3 to 5 minutes. Slowly whisk in vegetable stock, a little at a time, until a smooth sauce forms. Simmer 2 to 3 minutes until thickened. Season with soy sauce, salt and pepper. Serve as is, or pass it through a fine mesh strainer.

## Notes

- Wendy's soaking-dried-porcini method: soak 1/2 oz dried porcini in hot water, remove the mushrooms and save the broth, rinse the mushrooms before chopping, then chop and add to the mushroom/onion mix. Skim dirt from the top of the soaking water — it settles at the bottom of the dish.
- Reader tip (noted by Wendy): add a couple tablespoons of nutritional yeast dissolved in water, added after the broth. Also considered: miso powder if on hand.
- 11.25.25: let simmer to thicken longer than 3 minutes.
- 11.26.18: added a minced garlic clove and soaked 1/2 oz dried porcini — "Excellent!"
- 11.20.18: used 2 tablespoons tamari (didn't have soy sauce) plus 2 tablespoons nutritional yeast dissolved in 1/4 cup warm water; 1/4-1/3 cup oil (1/2 cup was too much).
- Wendy's private note: better than Bouillon + a box of Trader Joe's veg stock; 1/2 oz dried porcini (or fresh, but then no broth).
- Makes a lot of gravy — could cut the recipe in half, but Wendy notes "Don't."
