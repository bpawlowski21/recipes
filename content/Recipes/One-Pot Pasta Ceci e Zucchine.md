## Ingredients

- 2 tablespoon olive oil, plus more for drizzling
- 1/2 cup yellow onion, cut into small dice (Wendy's addition: 1 clove chopped garlic)
- 2 cups zucchini, cut into a medium dice
- 1 can chick peas, drained and rinsed, about 1.5 cups
- 1 litre warm water, divided (approximate amount) (Wendy's substitution: use warm veggie broth)
- 175 grams small pasta, ditalini or conchigliette
- 2 tablespoon parmigiano reggiano, grated
- salt and pepper to taste
- 2 teaspoon lemon zest, optional
- 4 leaves fresh basil, hand-torn, optional

## Directions

1. In a large pan with high sides, add the olive oil over medium heat.
2. Once the olive oil is warmed through, add in the onion and garlic (Wendy's addition). Cook until it's golden and fragrant, about 5 minutes.
3. Add in the zucchini and chick peas, mix them in with the onion and olive oil. Season with salt and pepper.
4. Add about 2 cups of warm veggie broth (Wendy's substitution for the warm water) — just enough to cover the zucchini and chick peas.
5. Let the zucchini and chick peas cook for 5-7 minutes.
6. Remove about 1 1/4 cups of zucchini into a measuring cup and blend with an immersion blender until completely blended.
7. Add the blended zucchini and chick peas back into the pan. Adjust for salt and pepper.
8. Once the liquid comes back to a boil, add in the pasta.
9. Cook the pasta. When the pasta starts drying out, add more warm water one ladle at a time. Continue this process until the pasta is cooked to your liking.
   You will have to keep stirring the pasta often while it's cooking, or else it will stick to the bottom of the pan.
10. Remove from heat, and add grated parmigiano reggiano, and drizzle with extra olive oil. Add in lemon zest and basil if using. Stir once more.
11. Plate, top with more olive oil and parmigiano if desired. Buon Appetito!

## Notes

- Wendy's handwritten adaptations: sauté a clove of chopped garlic in with the onion (not in the printed ingredient list), and substitute warm vegetable broth for the plain warm water called for in the printed recipe.
